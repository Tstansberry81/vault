# The Tilting Picture: On God, Control, and a Divided Home

A personal essay in which Trav traces a small, recurring observation — a family portrait that keeps tilting toward his mom at his dad's house — into a reckoning with God, his parents' contrasting characters, and years of accumulated change he never had the tools to process.

## Key points
- Trav has felt a lifelong need for control rooted in childhood, manifesting as anxiety and discomfort in environments he cannot own or organize — particularly his room at his dad's house
- The tilting portrait functions as the essay's central symbol: a physical anomaly that becomes evidence, or at least provocation, for belief in a higher power
- Trav draws a sharp moral contrast between his parents — his mom as radically honest, his dad as a self-mythologizer whose words and actions don't align
- God is framed not as absent but as present and indifferent — an entity that may exist yet repeatedly failed to give Trav relief during a cascade of destabilizing life changes
- The catalogue of changes (new sibling, parent's young remarriage, boarding school for his brother Seaton, new baby, dog's death) reads as a trauma inventory he coped with through emotional suppression and rumination rather than processing
- The essay ends in defiant anger toward God rather than disbelief — a theism of grievance rather than faith

## Entities & concepts
- [[Mom]] — Trav's primary attachment figure; defined by radical honesty and reliability; the portrait's lean toward her mirrors Trav's own emotional lean
- [[Dad]] — lives separately, visited twice a month; characterized by hyperbole and self-aggrandizement; Trav consciously tries to learn from his words, not his behavior
- [[Seaton]] — Trav's younger brother, sent to boarding school; one node in the cascade of changes Trav struggled to absorb
- [[God]] — reframed here not as a comfort but as a powerful, indifferent force; Trav arrives at conditional belief paired with explicit resentment
- [[Control]] — a core psychological theme for Trav; identified as a trait since childhood, linked to anxiety when environments feel unowned
- [[Emotional suppression]] — Trav's named coping pattern: either forgetting entirely or ruminating until feeling is exhausted, described as hiding in "the cave of my own despair"
- [[Divided home / divorce]] — the structural backdrop of the essay; the uncomfortable room, the misplaced portrait, and the twice-monthly visits all stem from this

## Why it matters to Trav
This is one of the clearest first-person articulations of Trav's core psychological architecture — the need for control, the parent-alignment dynamic, and his relationship to faith and grievance — making it essential connective tissue for threads across identity, family, and spirituality notes.
