# Dad on Change and Morality (Apple Note)

A terse Apple Note — two linked observations about [[Porter Stansberry (father)|his father's]] belief system, delivered without hedging.

## The note, verbatim
> *"my dad believes that no one can change because it benefits him to believe that. also relates to him believing in objective morality, yet being an atheist"*

## Two claims, unpacked

### 1. "No one can change" — as motivated reasoning
Traveler isn't simply saying his father is a pessimist about human nature. He's diagnosing the **motive behind the belief**: his father holds the fixed-people view because it **excuses** his own behavior. If no one changes, then (a) his father can't be blamed for who he is, and (b) those who've been hurt by him can't reasonably expect anything different. The belief protects its holder. This is Traveler's sharpest psychological observation about his father — and the most structurally complex, because it doesn't just describe what Porter thinks; it explains *why* the belief is sticky.

> [!note] Calibration
> This is Traveler's interpretation, not a documented stance Porter has taken publicly. It is psychologically coherent but should be treated as his read, not confirmed fact.

### 2. Objective morality + atheism — the unresolved tension
He frames this as *related* — and it is: if no one can change, moral accountability collapses, which strains any objective moral framework anyway. But the sharper tension he's flagging is the classic grounding problem: **objective morality without God requires a non-theistic foundation** (Kantian categorical imperative, secular humanism, natural law via reason, evolutionary ethics). His father doesn't appear to have named or worked through any of these — he simply holds both convictions simultaneously. Traveler finds this inconsistent.

This links to his own position: he is also an atheist ([[Faith vs. Knowledge]]) but is *openly wrestling* with what morality looks like without God ([[Intellectual Interests]], [[Ten Commandments, Free Will, and Morality Without God (chat)]]). The difference is that Traveler treats the tension as a live problem to solve; he reads his father as not noticing the problem.
