# Sayonara Speech

A farewell speech delivered by [[Traveler Stansberry]] at the end of a 12-day Japan exchange trip organized through [[St. Paul's School]]. The speech exists in three forms side-by-side in the same Apple Note: **English**, **hiragana-only Japanese** (no kanji — likely the form he read aloud), and **romaji transliteration**. The three-version format suggests deliberate preparation and likely some outside coaching or self-correction.

## Context
- **Trip organizers:** "Sensei Julian" (faculty lead, US side) and "Mr. Kodaira" (Japanese side coordinator); "Mrs. Wack" (additional chaperone/volunteer).
- **Host school:** not named in the speech, but cross-referenced with [[IB Japanese (SL)]] as Rikkyo Niiza High School.
- **Duration:** 12 days.
- **Delivery setting:** a morning assembly or farewell ceremony ("Good morning to everyone").

## Host family: the Moritas
- Traveler stayed with the **Morita family** for the duration of the trip.
- He expresses a specific hope that **Souta** (his host "buddy"/son) will one day visit America so he can "reciprocate the kindness and grace."
- Memories cited: playing **Jenga**, watching **movies**, and **weekend sightseeing** — deliberately small-scale, domestically warm.
- Tone is genuinely affectionate, not performatively polite.

## Buddies (Japanese student counterparts)
Three named: **Tomohiro**, **Ichigo**, and **Eon** — described as "very helpful during class in communicating my thoughts."

## Language notes
- The hiragana version is written **without kanji** throughout, including words where kanji would be standard (e.g., 「友人」 is romanized as *yūjin* but the hiragana body uses ともひろ, いちご, えおん in katakana-equivalents). This is consistent with a speech he wanted to **read aloud without looking up characters mid-delivery**.
- One orthographic inconsistency: the hiragana body writes 「かんしや」 (kansiya) instead of 「かんしゃ」 (kansha) at the closing thanks — a small romanization slip, not a conceptual error.
- The romaji is polished and accurate; the hiragana body has minor inconsistencies (expected for a self-transcribed draft).
- Bilingual delivery (English first, then Japanese) demonstrates real functional proficiency, not just symbolic gesture.

## Thematic significance
- Reinforces the [[IB Japanese (SL)]] throughline: Japanese as a "part of my life since kindergarten," not just a school subject.
- One of the most **emotionally open** documents in the corpus — warmer and less guarded than most essays. The emphasis is on *people and small moments*, not achievement.
- Connects to [[Emotional Life and Inner World]]: he values intimacy over spectacle, and names small domestic details (Jenga, movies) as his most lasting memories.
- Echoes the "outsider finding belonging" pattern also present in [[When I Feel Like an Outsider]] and [[College Essay (Akihabara)]] (the Akihabara essay is directly Japan-adjacent).
