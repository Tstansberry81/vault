## Topic clusters

### 1. The Adams-Onís (Transcontinental) Treaty, 1819
- Spain ceded East and West Florida; renounced all claims to the Pacific Northwest.
- U.S. renounced claims to Texas; agreed to pay $5 million in citizen claims against Spain.
- Defined the western limits of the Louisiana Territory, effectively extending nominal U.S. sovereignty to the Pacific Ocean.
- Unger frames it as Monroe's masterstroke: "In sending Jackson to seize Florida, Monroe had seized almost the entire continent."
- Natural-defense logic: Monroe valued boundaries that leaned on geographic barriers (Great Lakes, St. Lawrence, Gulf of Mexico, Rockies, Pacific).
- Source: Unger, p. 293.

### 2. The Monroe Doctrine (1823)
- Context: Russia's 1821 *ukaz* asserting Pacific Northwest claims; Holy Alliance (France, Austria, Russia, Prussia) backing the Troppau principle; France's April 1823 intervention in Spain to restore Ferdinand VII.
- British foreign minister Canning offered a joint Anglo-American declaration warning the Holy Alliance away from Spanish America; Secretary Adams rejected joint action, fearing British duplicity.
- The Doctrine as authored: **Adams** solely wrote the noncolonization paragraph; deeply influenced the two nonintervention paragraphs. Adams later said the noncolonization clause was "a warning to Great Britain herself."
- The Monroe cabinet oscillated between confidence in republican ideals and anxiety about U.S. weakness; the Doctrine was a statement of principles, not a binding commitment.
- Later use: Lincoln/Seward avoided invoking the Doctrine explicitly during the French intervention in Mexico (1861–67) to avoid antagonizing European powers. By the 20th century it had become a symbol of American ascendancy.
- Source: Sexton, pp. 44, 49–50, 52–53, 55, 148–149, 244.

### 3. John Adams's Presidency: Alien & Sedition Acts and the Quasi-War
- **Naturalization Act (1798):** residence requirement for citizenship raised from 5 to 14 years.
- **Alien Friends Act:** president could deport any alien deemed dangerous.
- **Alien Enemies Act:** permitted deportation of aliens from enemy nations during wartime.
- **Sedition Act (1798):** criminalized "seditious libel" — immediately used against the Republican press (Ben Bache, Matthew Lyon, John Daly Burk).
- Adams **never invoked** the Alien Act despite pressure from Secretary of State Pickering.
- **XYZ Affair:** three secret Talleyrand agents (X, Y, Z) demanded a $250,000 bribe plus a $10M loan before negotiations could proceed. Adams released the dispatches to Congress on March 5 (undecoded first by mistake).
- **Quasi-War (1798–1800):** undeclared naval conflict with France; Adams called it the "Half-War." Complicated by Hamilton's faction within his own cabinet.
- **Convention of 1800 / Treaty of Môrtefontaine:** ended the Quasi-War; established peace and neutral trading rights, but deferred indemnification for seized American property and the question of the formal Franco-American alliance.
- Sources: Ferling, pp. 365, 367, 372, 408; McCullough, pp. 483–485, 495, 505, 508.
