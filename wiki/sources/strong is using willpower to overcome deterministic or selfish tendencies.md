# strong is using willpower to overcome deterministic or selfish tendencies

One-sentence Apple Note. Full text verbatim:

> *"strong is using willpower to overcome deterministic or selfish tendencies"*

---

## What it says

A compressed ethical thesis in eleven words. The sentence makes three claims simultaneously:

1. **Strength is defined volitionally** — being strong *is* the act of exercising willpower, not a trait you passively possess.
2. **Determinism is assumed real** — "deterministic tendencies" are the default; they are not denied or argued away.
3. **Selfishness is bracketed with determinism** — selfish impulses are treated as being in the same category as determined ones: raw forces the will must overcome.

This is not a note about literary analysis — it's a note about *how he wants to live*.

---

## Why it matters

This is [[Traveler Stansberry]]'s **personal resolution** to the free-will question he wrestles with across four years of literary essays:

- In [[Iliad Essay]] (Grade 11): *"free will in the story is a fallacy"* — hard determinism.
- In [[Narrow Road to the Deep North Response]] (Grade 12): the *belief* in free will gives life meaning, even if it's an illusion.
- Here (private): determinism is real *and* willpower matters — the two coexist. Strength is what the gap between them looks like in practice.

The note is the philosophical engine behind [[Self-Discipline and Goals]]: the 75 Hard regimens, "lock in," screen limits, and behavioral rules aren't just habits — they are his *implementation* of this thesis.
