# Overview

This wiki is built from **[[Traveler Stansberry]]'s body of work — initially English coursework (2022–2026), now expanding to include technical/systems projects (2026).**

## Part 1: English Coursework (2022–2026)

**Scope:** 77 documents spanning four courses ([[English Curriculum 2022-2026|the curriculum]]): essays, responses, quizzes, study guides, memoirs, and one original short story. Sources are mostly Traveler's own writing, so the wiki doubles as a portrait of an evolving thinker.

### The evolving intellectual thesis

Across four years and ~25 literary works, a consistent intellectual fingerprint emerges. Traveler returns, again and again, to a small set of questions:

1. **Do we actually choose anything?** — [[Fate and Free Will]] is his signature preoccupation, from the [[The Iliad|Iliad]] ("free will… is a fallacy") to [[The Narrow Road to the Deep North]] ("the belief in free will is more advantageous for survival").
2. **What happens to a self under pressure?** — [[Identity and Its Collapse|identity]] crumbles under coercion, poverty, war, grief. By senior year, this becomes his dominant theme.
3. **Can individuals resist society?** — [[Individual vs. Society|The rebel loses]]. Power systems are older, stronger, and pitiless.
4. **What is power, and who should have it?** — [[Power, Hierarchy, and Justice|Power corrupts its seeker]], even (or especially) the righteous.
5. **Does art save us?** — [[Art as Survival|Art is survival]], not luxury — a basic human need even in camps, prisons, and ruins.

### Intellectual evolution (9th → 12th)

- **9th grade:** Fate-vs-free-will argument, somewhat surface-level; attraction to speculative fiction ([[Do Androids Dream of Electric Sheep?]]).
- **10th grade:** Thematic depth increases; civil disobedience and personal conscience become central ([[Civil Disobedience]], [[Frederick Douglass]]).
- **HL years (11th–12th):** Heavy European literature; existential and moral questions; direct engagement with trauma, slavery, war. The intellectual confidence grows — longer essays, sustained argument, willingness to contradict canonical interpretations.

### Core convictions

His writing across four years reveals several deep convictions:
- Skepticism about institutions (schools, governments, markets, churches)
- Belief in individual conscience and integrity
- Realism about power (you can't overcome it; you can only understand it)
- Sensitivity to suffering and its moral weight
- Fascination with how people create meaning under duress

See [[Core Convictions]] and [[Intellectual Evolution]] for full synthesis.
