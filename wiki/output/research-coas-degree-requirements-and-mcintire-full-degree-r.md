## COMPREHENSIVE PATHWAY ANALYSIS: UVA CoAS TO McINTIRE SCHOOL OF COMMERCE

### **I. UVA COLLEGE OF ARTS AND SCIENCES (CoAS) DEGREE REQUIREMENTS**

#### **A. Total Credit Requirements**

All students must pass a minimum of 120 credits of approved courses.
Of the 120 credits required for the degree, no fewer than 108 credits must be from courses taught inside the College of Arts and Sciences or College-equivalent courses from transfer or test credits.
The remaining 12 credits may be College elective courses or elective courses from outside the College.


**Application for student with 14 transfer credits:**
- With 14 transfer credits + physics exemption (likely 4 credits), student enters with approximately 18 credits counted toward the 120 minimum
- Must complete approximately 102 additional credits at UVA (18 + 102 = 120)
- Must complete at least 94 more credits in CoAS courses (108 total required - 14 transfer = 94 at UVA)

#### **B. General Education Requirements**

**New College Curriculum (for students entering Fall 2020 or later):**

1. **Engagements (Intellectual seminars)**: Variable credits, taken in first year

2. **Literacies (Core Skills)**:
   - 
Both the First Writing and Second Writing requirements comprise the Rhetoric for the 21st Century component of the new curriculum. All undergraduate students meet the First Writing requirement by successfully completing one of the requirement-eligible ENWR courses as determined by placement. All undergraduate students must complete at UVA the Second Writing requirement by enrolling in an identified Second Writing course with a grade of C- or better.

   - 
All students in the College must satisfy the World Language Requirement by reaching the 2020 (advanced intermediate) level or equivalent.


3. **Disciplines**: 
Students in the College Curriculum must earn 3 credits in each of the seven Discipline categories. Courses that count towards the Disciplines must be taken from six different departments.


**Note on Transfer Credits and Exemptions:**
- 
Once a student matriculates, they must complete all general education requirements with College of Arts and Science courses.

- Physics exemption satisfies one science Discipline requirement
- 14 transfer credits may fulfill some Discipline and Engagement requirements if they meet course equivalencies, but many general education requirements must be completed at UVA

#### **C. Major Requirements**

College students must complete the requirements for one major to receive the B.A. or the B.S.
Generally, all major and minor requirements must be completed with courses taken at UVA. Exceptions must be approved by the major / minor department.


Major requirements vary by field but typically require 12-18 upper-level courses (36-54+ credits).

#### **D. Academic Performance Standards**

To earn a degree, a student must have earned a grade point average of at least 2.0 on all graded courses taken at UVA and offered for a degree.


#### **E. Time to Degree**

Generally, College students must complete their degree in 8 full-time semesters (correspondingly less for transfer students).

### **II. MCINTIRE SCHOOL OF COMMERCE — ADMISSION PATHWAY**

#### **A. First-Year Application Timeline**

Students who are in their first year can apply to the program in the spring semester of their first year of classes. If accepted, they will start the program in August of their second year.


#### **B. McIntire Admission Prerequisites**

The admissions requirements for first-year students include prerequisite courses that must be completed within either semester of their first year. In addition, students should continue to make progress on UVA's General Education Requirements.
Before entering the McIntire School of Commerce, you must complete a minimum of 54 credits and complete all prerequisites by the fall of your third year. This includes any test credits, transfer credits, and courses taken at UVA.


**Key Prerequisite Courses (to complete by spring of first year for fall third-year entry):**
- COMM 2010: Introduction to Financial Accounting
- COMM 2020: Introduction to Managerial Accounting
- ECON 2010 & ECON 2020: Principles of Economics (Micro & Macro)
- STAT 1120 or equivalent: Statistics (Note: 
AP or transfer credit for STAT 1120 will no longer satisfy this requirement
)
- Mathematics requirement (depends on placement)
- Foreign Language requirement (as defined by CoAS)
- First Writing and Humanities/Engagements (as defined by CoAS)

**Transfer Credit Application:**

Generally an Introduction to Business course and Computer Applications course will be accepted as meeting the requirement.
 However, specific courses like accounting and economics typically must be completed at UVA.

#### **C. McIntire Admission Evaluation**

Factors taken into consideration include academic performance (GPA, performance in courses related to business, degree of difficulty of courses, and course load taken); co-curricular activities including volunteer and work experience; and evidence of solid communication, teamwork, leadership, and personal skills. Students must have both solid academic and co-curricular performance and display a level of maturity to show they will contribute to the McIntire classroom.

### **III. MCINTIRE SCHOOL OF COMMERCE — B.S. DEGREE REQUIREMENTS**

#### **A. Program Duration and Structure**

The McIntire School's B.S. in Commerce degree is a full-time program (three years for the new undergraduate design and two years for this year's enrolling third-year class). Students are not allowed to complete the Commerce degree in less than six fall/spring semesters, nor are they allowed to remain for more than six fall/spring semesters.


#### **B. McIntire Core Curriculum (New 3-Year Format)**

**Year 1 (2nd Year at UVA) — Foundational Core (15 credits)**


The Foundational Core (15 credits) is designed to ensure that all McIntire students build a strong base of analytical, communication, and decision-making skills at the start of their Commerce education. Integrating key business fundamentals in accounting, finance, data analysis, communication, and technology, this sequenced, skills-building progression across the second year ensures that every student is well-prepared for the integrated, team-based consulting projects in their third year.


Includes:
- Financial and Managerial Accounting (2 courses, 6 credits)
- Economics (2 courses, 6 credits)
- Data Analytics and Technology course(s)
- Communication/presentation skills

**Year 2 (3rd Year at UVA) — Integrated Core Experience (ICE)**


This immersive curriculum during your third year combines all the core disciplines for a wide-ranging, hands-on approach to business education. Taught by a dedicated faculty team, small student groups collaborate to solve real-world business problems that they present to corporate sponsor executives. By the spring of your third year, you'll have the broad-based liberal arts foundation and applied business knowledge that will help you choose your concentration or functional area of study.


**Year 3 (4th Year at UVA) — Concentration & Electives**

#### **C. Concentrations Available**

Students may choose to concentrate in Finance, Accounting, Marketing, Management, or Information Technology.


Each concentration requires a sequence of specialized courses (typically 12-15 credits for concentration-specific requirements).

#### **D. Total Commerce School Credits**
Students must complete 39+ required Commerce hours during enrollment, which includes:
- 15 credits: Foundational Core
- ICE coursework (varies, typically 12-15 credits)
- Concentration requirements (12-15 credits)
- Commerce electives to reach 39+ total


COMM 3410 Commercial Law may be taken before enrollment and is included in students' 39 required Commerce hours if taken at UVA.


#### **E. Electives and Double Majors**

The McIntire curriculum allows for electives to be taken inside or outside the Commerce School. For the Commerce Class of 2021, 51% of Commerce students completed a second major or minor outside the McIntire School.
In addition to a concentration, you'll have the option to choose up to two of six tracks—an area of specialty that spans across several disciplines or delves more deeply into a concentration—to prepare you for the workplace. These tracks allow you to further specialize in an area that augments Integrated Core Curriculum (ICE) and concentration coursework.
