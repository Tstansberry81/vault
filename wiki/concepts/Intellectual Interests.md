# Intellectual Interests

[[Traveler Stansberry]]'s private philosophical musings — short notes-to-self that show the same mind as his essays, thinking on its own time. They cluster around four questions:

## 1. Free will vs. determinism
His most striking private note defines his ethics directly: *"strong is using willpower to overcome deterministic or selfish tendencies."* This is the **personal counterpart** to his literary evolution on [[Fate and Free Will]] — and to the systems he builds for himself in [[Self-Discipline and Goals]]. He has, in effect, resolved the Iliad-vs-Narrow-Road tension in his own life: determinism is real, but willpower is the act of resisting it.

**Coursework connection:** This theme intersects directly with [[Kant]]'s concept of autonomy and the rational will. Currently reading *Grounding for the Metaphysics of Morals* (Section I, due Sep 15) in [[Moral and Political Philosophy (UVA Fall 2026)]].

## 2. Morality
A standing preoccupation: a note simply reading *"something to do with figuring out morality"*; interest in **secular humanism, Kantian rationalism, virtue ethics**; and the sharp observation that his father "believes in objective morality, yet [is] an atheist — paradox."

This question now has **formal institutional scaffolding:** [[Moral and Political Philosophy (UVA Fall 2026)]] examines foundational ethical frameworks through close reading of Aristotle, Kant, and (likely) modern utilitarians. The reading assignments directly target the same problem he's been privately puzzling over.

## 3. Religion and epistemology
Core tension: **can knowledge exist without faith?** He's skeptical of religious tradition (see [[Faith vs. Knowledge]]) yet intellectually curious about why it persists and what it claims to answer. Private notes show curiosity about the EPR thought experiment, quantum mechanics, and cosmology — exploring the boundary between the physical and the metaphysical.

## 4. Technology, AI, and consciousness
Questions about machine consciousness, the nature of intelligence, and the relationship between computation and meaning. Evident in his [[Homework Hatch (startup)]] work and in his quant modeling projects.

## Integration: The Bridge from Private to Public Philosophy

Until Fall 2026, these interests remained **private**—explored in notes-to-self, informal chats with ChatGPT, and implicitly in his essays. Now, through [[Moral and Political Philosophy (UVA Fall 2026)]], his intellectual curiosity has formal structure and peer engagement (seminar discussion, exams, canonical texts).

**Observation:** His interests are **deeply coherent**. The free-will preoccupation, the morality problem, and his systems-building (RESOLVE, quant models) all point to one deep question: **How do rational agents construct meaning and agency in a determined or indifferent universe?** Kant's answer—through the transcendental subject and the categorical imperative—is philosophically aligned with where Traveler's thinking already points.
