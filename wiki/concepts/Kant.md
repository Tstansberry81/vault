## Core Ideas

### Deontological Ethics (Moral Philosophy)

Kant's ethical theory is **deontological** (duty-focused, not consequence-focused). Key principles:

1. **Categorical Imperative (CI):** The supreme principle of morality. Acts are right iff they could be universal laws. Often formulated as "act only according to that maxim whereby you can at the same time will that it should become a universal law."
   - **First formulation:** Universal Law formulation (above)
   - **Second formulation:** Humanity formulation — "Act in such a way that you treat humanity, whether in your own person or in the person of any other, never merely as a means to an end, but always at the same time as an end."
   - **Third formulation:** Kingdom of Ends formulation

2. **Duty (Pflicht):** Moral action stems from duty to the moral law, not from inclination, emotion, or self-interest. **Only actions performed from duty have moral worth.**

3. **Rationality and the Will:** Morality is grounded in reason and rational agency. A rational being is one who can recognize and act according to the moral law.

4. **Autonomy:** Freedom is the ability to govern oneself according to the moral law; heteronomy is dependence on external forces or inclinations.

## Sections of *Grounding for the Metaphysics of Morals*

- **Section I (Transition from ordinary moral knowledge to philosophical):** Introduces the Categorical Imperative; establishes that morality is rooted in duty, not consequence
- **Section II (Transition from popular moral philosophy to metaphysics of morals):** Develops the three formulations of the Categorical Imperative; explains concepts like duty, will, reason
- **Section III (Final step: from metaphysics of morals to critique of pure reason):** Addresses the problem of human freedom and autonomy in a causally determined universe
