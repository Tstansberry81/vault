# Immanuel Kant (1724–1804)

**German philosopher**; one of the central figures of the Enlightenment and the most influential philosopher of the modern era. **Kant is the subject of [[Traveler Stansberry]]'s Fall 2026 philosophy coursework, specifically his ethical theory.**

## Quick Reference

- **Era:** Enlightenment (late 18th century)
- **Key works:** *Critique of Pure Reason* (epistemology/metaphysics), *Critique of Practical Reason* (ethics), *Grounding for the Metaphysics of Morals* (ethics)
- **Core idea:** Knowledge, morality, and aesthetics are shaped by the structure of the human mind and rational agency; synthesis of rationalism and empiricism
- **Ethics:** Deontological (duty-based); the Categorical Imperative as the supreme moral principle

## Core Ideas

### Deontological Ethics (Moral Philosophy)

Kant's ethical theory is **deontological** (duty-focused, not consequence-focused). Key principles:

1. **Categorical Imperative:** The supreme principle of morality. Acts are right iff they could be universal laws. Often formulated as "act only according to that maxim whereby you can at the same time will that it should become a universal law."

2. **Autonomy:** Moral agents are rational beings with the capacity to give themselves moral law. Autonomy is the source of dignity.

3. **Duty:** Moral action flows from duty (recognition of the Categorical Imperative), not from inclination or consequence.

4. **Good Will:** The only unconditional good is the good will — the intention to act rightly from duty, independent of outcomes.

### Epistemology & Metaphysics

- **Synthesis:** Kant famously synthesized rationalism (Descartes, Leibniz) with empiricism (Hume), arguing that **knowledge is shaped by the structure of the human mind** (space, time, categories of understanding) while being grounded in sensory experience.
- **Phenomena vs. Noumena:** We know things as they appear to us (phenomena), not as they are in themselves (noumena).
- **Transcendental Idealism:** Space and time are not features of the world itself but forms of human intuition.

### Autonomy & Freedom

Kant's philosophy centers on **human autonomy**: the rational agent's capacity to be self-legislating (giving oneself moral law). Freedom and autonomy are inseparable; moral agency presupposes freedom.

## Historical Context

Kant lived through the Enlightenment and was shaped by the scientific revolution (Newton) and the challenge of radical skepticism (Hume). His philosophy aimed to explain how **reason and science are possible** while preserving space for freedom and morality — a central question of modern philosophy.
