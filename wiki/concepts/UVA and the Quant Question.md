# UVA and the Quant Question

The unresolved decision: will [[Traveler Stansberry]] pursue **quantitative finance** as a career, or will he pivot to something else?

## The setup

- **College:** Committed to [[College Search|UVA McIntire School of Commerce]], Finance major (matriculating July 2026)
- **Math foundation:** IB Math HL-track student (strong theoretical math); exploring higher math (differential equations, complex analysis) via self-study
- **Quant evidence:** Demonstrated capability
  - Designed and deployed a [[Personal Quant Model|multi-factor equity model]] (conceptual design ✓, math ✓, **code: AI-assisted, not hand-written**) 
  - Built a product stack around it ([[The Edge (trading model)|momentum/acceleration trader]], [[Vision (Porter Intelligence)|consumer dashboard]])
  - Actively studies quantitative finance (derivatives, no-arbitrage, volatility, asset pricing) via chats with Claude
  - Reads finance literature (RenTech, Renaissance Technologies, etc.)

- **Open gaps:** 
  - **Coding proficiency unclear.** Models are AI-coded; he directs but hasn't proven he can hand-write production code.
  - **Depth vs. breadth.** Can discuss quant concepts fluently but hasn't shown mastery of *implementation* (numerical methods, backtesting rigor, live trading).
  - **Commitment depth.** Quant finance is a *serious* career choice requiring 2+ years of intense math/CS before employment; unclear if he's willing to pay that cost vs. taking a lighter path in finance.

## The decision points

By the end of first year at UVA (spring 2027), [[Traveler Stansberry]] will likely need to decide:
1. **Double major:** Finance + Math (or CS)? Doing quant seriously means building both pillars.
2. **Internship track:** Quant fund, prop trading, or traditional asset management?
3. **Summer projects:** Use summers to build real trading systems, or explore other domains?

The answer depends on whether his *ideas* translate into *skill* once he's embedded in serious coursework (upper-level math, CS, graduate-level finance).

## Calibration

> [!warning] Aspirational vs. Demonstrated  
> Traveler is **capable** of pursuing quant (ideas ✓, some math ✓, design judgment ✓).  
> He is **not yet proven** at implementation (code gaps, untested backtesting, no live-trading record).  
> The "question" is whether he'll close those gaps and commit to the 2–3 year payoff before he can get hired.  
> Don't conflate "can do" with "will do."
