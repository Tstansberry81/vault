# Opposite Action (DBT)

A skill from **Dialectical Behaviour Therapy (DBT)** — the practice of acting directly *opposite* to the action urged by a difficult or unhelpful emotion, in order to change the emotional experience itself.

## How it works

| Emotion / Urge | Opposite Action |
|---|---|
| Shame → hide, withdraw | Be visible, engage, participate |
| Fear → avoid | Approach the feared situation |
| Anger → attack | Be kind or disengage |
| Sadness → isolate | Reach out, activate |

The logic: emotions partly sustain themselves through the behaviors they produce. Acting opposite interrupts that feedback loop.

## How it shows up in Traveler's life

[[Traveler Stansberry]] noted this skill after a session with **Dave** (inferred: a therapist or counselor). The specific prescription recorded: *"enthusiasm and participate 100% initially."*

That framing — full, enthusiastic engagement at the *start* — maps onto his documented pattern of **emotional withdrawal and detachment** in social situations: he describes wanting to "exit" social settings, feeling "distant from humanity," and going through the motions (see [[Emotional Life and Inner World]]). The opposite-action directive is a concrete counter-instruction: don't wait to feel engaged — *act* engaged first, fully, from the top.

> [!note] The note is brief. Whether Traveler practiced this consistently, or whether it was a one-session takeaway, is unknown. Recorded plainly as what the evidence shows: he was aware of the skill and got a specific instruction around it.
