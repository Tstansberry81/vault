# Secular Humanism (concept)

The cluster of **post-religious moral frameworks** that [[Traveler Stansberry]] has shown interest in — noted together in a private Apple Note ([[secular humanism (Apple Note)]]) as **secular humanism, Kantian rationalism, and virtue ethics**.

This page covers the frameworks themselves and the evidence for Traveler's engagement with each. See [[Intellectual Interests]] for the broader map of his philosophical musings.

---

## The three frameworks

### Secular humanism
The view that human reason and dignity — not divine command — are the foundation of ethics and meaning. Associated with the Enlightenment and modern humanist organizations (American Humanist Association, etc.). Compatible with atheism; emphasizes science, individual rights, and compassion.

**Relevance to Traveler:** His [[Faith vs. Knowledge]] position — technology liberates from religion; faith ≠ fact — is structurally humanist. He concedes religion is useful for those "without strong foundations based on education and moral standards" ([[Self-Reliance (essay)|Self-Reliance response]]), implying education/reason as the proper substitute. The label "secular humanism" names this position explicitly.

### Kantian rationalism (deontology)
Immanuel Kant's moral theory: right action is determined by universal rational principles (the **categorical imperative** — act only according to maxims you could will to be universal laws). Consequences are irrelevant; what matters is the rational form of the act. Contrasted with consequentialism (utilitarianism) and situational virtue ethics.

**Relevance to Traveler:** He reached for this framework in [[Naming the Rigid-Rule Philosophy and Intelligence vs Curiosity (chat)]], probing "the philosophy that says rules have to be rigid" before landing on deontology / Kant's categorical imperative. He tested it against the apple-thief dilemma and found it unsatisfying as a standalone rule (too rigid, ignores intent/context) — suggesting critical engagement, not simple endorsement.

### Virtue ethics
The Aristotelian view that ethics is about cultivating *character* — virtues like courage, honesty, practical wisdom — rather than following rules or maximizing outcomes. Right action flows from a virtuous person acting from good character.

**Relevance to Traveler:** His private note that *"strong is using willpower to overcome deterministic or selfish tendencies"* ([[Intellectual Interests]] §1) is essentially a virtue-ethics formulation — self-mastery and character over impulse. His [[Self-Discipline and Goals]] practices (75 Hard, etc.) are the behavioral correlate. The Civic Engagement Club debrief ([[Morality, Policy, and the Government - Civic Engagement Club Debrief (chat)|chat]]) also surfaces virtue-adjacent reasoning.

## The underlying question

All three frameworks appear in one note because they address the same problem Traveler faces: **how to have a serious, grounded ethics without religion**. His [[Faith vs. Knowledge]] skepticism removes divine command as a moral anchor; secular humanism, Kantian rationalism, and virtue ethics are three candidate replacements.

> [!warning] Interest ≠ mastery
> The Apple Note is three words — there is no evidence Traveler has read Kant, Aristotle, or humanist primary texts. He has encountered these *labels* and finds them interesting. His engagement is at the level of curious recognition, not philosophical fluency. This is consistent with how he approached deontology in the [[Naming the Rigid-Rule Philosophy and Intelligence vs Curiosity (chat)|Naming chat]] — reaching for vocabulary, testing it on a hypothetical, not deploying it systematically.
