# Film and TV

[[Traveler Stansberry]]'s taste in film and television — documented in his personal writing and referenced in his index. Distinct from his [[Reading List]]; this page covers moving-image media. His taste runs toward **moral complexity, psychological depth, and auteur-driven work** well beyond mainstream teen fare.

---

## The antihero pattern — his own explanation

The clearest account is in [[Halloween (personal essay)]]:

> "I like TV shows. I always have. Particularly, ones about morally, ethically, and psychologically compromised characters. Breaking Bad, Hannibal, Dexter; the list goes on. I did not really know why until I figured it out: I liked seeing bad people try to do good things."

He decodes each show:
- ***Breaking Bad*** — "a cancer victim become a drug dealer" (Walter White's moral descent)
- ***Dexter*** — "a serial killer act based on his 'code'" (violence governed by a personal moral system)
- ***Hannibal*** — "a mentally insane autistic man solve crimes with his incredible empathy" (Will Graham; the show he specifically names)

His explanation is personal, not aesthetic: he wants the villains to turn good because **that is the question he is asking about himself** — and his father. The TV preference is a proxy for his own moral self-assessment. From the [[Emotional Detachment, Antihero TV, and His Father (chat)|Nov 1 ChatGPT confessional]]:

> He wants Walter White, Dexter, and Will Graham to "win" because he wants proof that someone "most compromised in their own humanity" can still be redeemed — i.e. proof *he* can be.

He closes the Halloween essay with a frank admission: "TV is fiction. The bad guys do not get to be the heroes; not in the real world anyway. I am just honestly trying to figure out which side I am on, and it's extremely hard to tell."
