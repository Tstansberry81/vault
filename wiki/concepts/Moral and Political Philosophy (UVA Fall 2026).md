# Moral and Political Philosophy (UVA Fall 2026)

A philosophy seminar in [[Traveler Stansberry]]'s first semester at the [[University of Virginia]]. The course examines foundational questions in ethics and political philosophy through close reading of canonical texts, culminating in two exams.

## Course Structure

- **Format:** Twice-weekly seminars (regular meeting slot: 3:30–4:20 PM)
- **Assessment:** Exam-based (no essays, papers, or projects in this syllabus)
- **Exams:**
  - **First Exam:** Tuesday, September 29, 3:30–4:20 PM
  - **Second Exam:** Thursday, November 5, 3:30–4:20 PM

## Reading Schedule & Topics

### Week 1 (Aug 25 & 27) — Why Moral and Political Philosophy?
- **Readings:**
  - Plato, *Apology*
  - Rachels, "The Challenge of Cultural Relativism"
- **Focus:** The foundations of ethical inquiry; the role of reason and tradition in moral knowledge

### Week 2 (Sep 1 & 3) — Aristotle's Question: Ethics, Happiness, and Virtue
- **Readings:** Aristotle, *Nicomachean Ethics*, Books I–III.4
- **Focus:** The concept of eudaimonia (happiness/flourishing); virtue ethics and the good life

### Week 3 (Sep 8 & 10) — Aristotle on the Particular Virtues and Friendship
- **Readings:** Aristotle, *Nicomachean Ethics*, Books III.4–V; VIII–IX
- **Focus:** The individual virtues (courage, temperance, justice); philia (friendship) as central to human flourishing

### Week 4 (Sep 15 & 17) — Kant's Deontology: Duty, Autonomy, and the Categorical Imperative
- **Readings:** Immanuel Kant, *Grounding for the Metaphysics of Morals* (a.k.a. *Groundwork*), Section I
- **Status:** Reading assigned Sep 12; due **Monday, Sep 15** (flagged as "Not Started" in [[RESOLVE Daily Activity 2026-09-12|RESOLVE activity log]] — recommended weekend priority)
- **Focus:** Kant's departure from virtue ethics; the concept of duty (Pflicht); the good will as the only unconditional good; introduction to the categorical imperative

### Subsequent Weeks (Sep 22 onward)
- **Topics to follow:** Kant's Sections II & III of *Grounding*; further deontological frameworks; possibly utilitarianism and other modern ethical theories
