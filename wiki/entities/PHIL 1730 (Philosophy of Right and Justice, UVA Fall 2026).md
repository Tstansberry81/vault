## Kant's Deontology (Preliminary Notes)

[[Kant]]'s deontological ethics are central to contemporary moral philosophy and are characterized by:
- **Duty-based:** Morality is grounded in rational duty, not consequences
- **Categorical Imperative (CI):** The supreme principle of morality; acts are right iff their maxims can be universalized as law
- **Autonomy:** Moral agents are free rational beings who give themselves moral law
- **Common-sense morality:** The CI formalizes ordinary moral intuitions rather than inventing new morality

**Grounding, Section I** typically covers the move from common-sense moral notions (good will, duty, etc.) to the concept of the Categorical Imperative, establishing that **duty and the CI are latent in ordinary moral thinking**.
