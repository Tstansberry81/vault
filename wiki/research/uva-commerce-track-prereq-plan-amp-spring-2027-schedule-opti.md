## How the admissions track actually works
- Since AY 2024-25, **first-years apply to McIntire's three-year curriculum at the end of their spring semester**, matriculating into Commerce in fall of second year. ([McIntire admissions](https://www.commerce.virginia.edu/bs-commerce/admissions))
- **Timeline:** application opens in **March**, deadline **end of April**, decisions released in **June**. So my window is March–April 2027, decision June 2027.
- Second-years apply on the old cycle (opens October, deadline January). That's my fallback if the first-year attempt misses.
- Review is **holistic — no single factor guarantees admission**. Stated factors: GPA, performance in business-related courses, course difficulty, course load, co-curriculars/work/volunteer experience, and evidence of communication, teamwork, and leadership.
- Structural requirements: **minimum 54 credits** and prerequisites completed before entering McIntire; 120 credits total to graduate. AP/transfer credit for STAT 1120 does **not** satisfy the stats prereq — it has to be taken at UVA. First Writing, Humanities/Engagements, and the College's foreign language requirement are all in the prereq set. ([Eligibility](https://www.commerce.virginia.edu/bs-commerce/admissions/eligibility-high-school))

## What this means strategically
1. **First year IS the application.** Two semesters of grades, five-ish courses, and whatever leadership I've earned by April 2027. There is no time to fix a weak fall.
2. **Course load is graded, not just GPA.** A 4.0 off 12 easy credits reads worse than a 3.8 off 16 hard ones. Spring should be 15–16 credits.
3. **Stats must happen at UVA.** Fall 2027 at the latest, spring 2027 if it fits — earlier is safer.
4. **Leadership needs a title, not attendance.** Whatever I join in September has to be something I hold a position in by April. Joining eight orgs in March 2027 fools nobody.
5. **Keep a fallback major alive.** Roughly 1 in 3 to 1 in 2 applicants don't get in (CONFIRM current rate). If spring is 100% Commerce-flavored, a rejection costs a semester. CS + Econ is the natural hedge and overlaps heavily anyway.

## Spring 2027 — three schedule options
All assume 5 courses, ~15–16 credits, and all include ENWR (FWR) because I don't have it.

### Option A — Max Commerce signal (~15–16 cr)
| Course | Why |
|---|---|
| COMM foundations (CONFIRM #) | The prereq and the signal |
| ECON 2020 Macro | Completes the econ pair; pairs with fall Micro |
| STAT 1120 or 2120 | Prereq that can't be AP'd — kill it early |
| ENWR 1510 | FWR, required |

**Pros:** every single course is a prereq; nothing wasted; strongest possible transcript read in April.
**Cons:** zero hedge — a rejection leaves me with no progress toward a backup major. Also a heavy quantitative load stacked on a heavy fall.

### Option B — Hedged (~16 cr) ← my pick
| Course | Why |
|---|---|
| COMM foundations (CONFIRM #) | Prereq + signal |
| ENWR 1510 | FWR, required |
| Calculus II | Keeps CS/Econ/quant doors open, shows rigor |

**Cons:** defers STAT to fall 2027; hardest of the three to pull a high GPA out of.

### Option C — GPA-protective (~15 cr)
| Course | Why |
|---|---|
| COMM foundations (CONFIRM #) | Prereq + signal |
| ENWR 1510 | FWR, required |
| STAT 1120 | Prereq, can't be AP'd |
| Japanese (next level) | Language prereq |
| Humanities/Engagements course | Knocks out a Gen Ed requirement, GPA cushion |

**Pros:** most realistic 3.9+ path; clears three prereq categories; lowest burnout risk after a heavy fall.
**Cons:** weakest rigor signal of the three; no fallback-major progress; the Engagements slot is the one a committee will read as filler.

## Decision rule
**Decide in late October 2026, off actual midterm grades in CS / Calc I / Micro.**
- Crushing all three (A/A-) → Option B. Rigor is affordable and the hedge is free.
- Struggling in Calc or CS → Option C. Protect the GPA; a 3.9 with a lighter spring beats a 3.5 with a heroic one.
- Comfortable but not dominant, and certain about Commerce → Option A.
