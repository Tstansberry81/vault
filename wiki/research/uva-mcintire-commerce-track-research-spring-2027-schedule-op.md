# UVA McIntire Commerce Track — Research & Spring 2027 Schedule Options

_Compiled 2026-07-30. Sources at bottom. Everything here is from McIntire's own pages plus one Cavalier Daily report; anything marked (est.) is inferred from the prior cycle, not published for 2027._

## 1. How the track actually works now

McIntire moved to a **three-year curriculum** starting with AY 2024–25. The old "apply at the end of second year" path is dead for Trav's cohort:

- First-years apply **at the end of their spring semester** (application opens ~March, deadline April/May, decisions June), and enroll in McIntire at the **start of second year**.
- Six semesters are then spent in McIntire: second- and third-year Integrated Core, then concentration work.
- Everyone majors in **Commerce** and picks a concentration: Accounting, Finance, Information Technology, Management, Marketing. Optional tracks on top: Business Analytics, Entrepreneurship, Global Commerce, Quantitative Finance, Real Estate, Strategic Brand Consulting & Communications.

## 2. Prerequisites — the whole list is three items

1. **COMM 1800 — Foundations of Commerce**
2. **ECON 2010 — Principles of Microeconomics**
3. **First Writing Requirement** (ENWR 1510/1520 or equivalent credit)

Prereqs may be completed in **either semester of the first year**, so a spring COMM 1800 is legitimate and expected — an in-progress prereq does not block the application.

What was *removed* in the reform (important, because older advice online still lists it):
- The two large online accounting courses (COMM 2010 / 2020) are gone — accounting is now taken in-person *inside* McIntire. COMM 2010 is no longer offered at all.
- The standalone statistics requirement is gone, replaced by a quantitative analysis course taken in second year.
- Most gen-ed prereqs were stripped out; students are only *advised* to keep making progress on General Education / Engagements.

Credit math for the degree: 120 total — 48 graded Commerce credits, 3 prerequisite credits, 15 second-year Integrated Core, 18 third-year Integrated Core + Commercial Law, 9–12 concentration, 0–3 Commerce elective, ~57 non-Commerce, plus 15 graded flex credits.

## 3. What actually decides the outcome

- **Academic performance**, with GPA explicitly described as only one factor.
- A **quantitative GPA** computed from prereq courses — econ, math, statistics. For Trav that means **Calc I and ECON 2010 this fall are the two highest-leverage grades of the year.** A B- in Calc I is a real cost, not a rounding error.
- **Co-curricular performance and maturity** — McIntire says applicants must show both, and evaluates against named qualities: academic excellence, intellectual curiosity, empathy, work ethic, agency, accountability, impact, and the ability to engage across different backgrounds.
- Essays/short answers carry the "human-centered mindset" framing McIntire repeats everywhere. Written communication is graded implicitly.

Practical read: with only three prereqs and no test score, the differentiators are **the quant GPA, the essays, and one or two real leadership/impact stories started in the fall of first year.** Clubs joined in April 2027 will read as decoration.

## 4. Trav's position going in

- ECON 2010 ✅ in progress fall 2026.
- Calc I ✅ (not required, but feeds the quant GPA and signals rigor — good).
- Intro CS — not required for Commerce, but genuinely useful if the concentration ends up IT, or the Business Analytics / Quantitative Finance track.
- **Open question:** is the First Writing Requirement already satisfied by AP/transfer credit? If not, it must land in spring 2027. Verify in SIS before enrollment opens.
- Missing pieces to confirm: College of Arts & Sciences **foreign language** requirement and Engagements progress — these don't gate the McIntire application but they do gate graduation and the fallback plan.

## 5. Three Spring 2027 schedule options

All three assume COMM 1800 is non-negotiable and target 15 credits.

### Option A — "McIntire-max" (highest signal, highest risk)
| Course | Cr | Why |
|---|---|---|
| COMM 1800 Foundations of Commerce | 3 | Required prereq |
| ENWR 1510 First Writing | 3 | Required prereq (skip if already satisfied → add Engagements course) |
| ECON 2020 Macroeconomics | 3 | Not required, but pure quant-GPA fuel and the natural sequel to micro |
| CS 2100 (Data Structures) | 3 | Keeps the analytics/IT concentration and quant-finance track credible |
| Language / Engagements | 3 | Chips at the College requirements |

**Best if:** fall grades come back strong and he wants the transcript to scream quantitative. **Risk:** ECON 2020 + CS 2100 in the same semester is the heaviest workload of the three, in the exact semester the application is being written.

### Option B — "Hedged dual-path" (recommended default)
| Course | Cr | Why |
|---|---|---|
| COMM 1800 | 3 | Required prereq |
| ENWR 1510 First Writing | 3 | Required prereq |
| MATH 1320 Calculus II | 3 | Continues the calc sequence; keeps CS/Econ majors fully open as a fallback if McIntire says no |
| CS 2100 | 3 | Same hedge, from the other direction |
| Language / Engagements | 3 | Graduation requirement progress |

**Best if:** he wants a real Plan B. If McIntire rejects, he lands in second year with the CS or Econ major already on track and zero lost time. **Cost:** no ECON 2020, so slightly less econ depth on the application — which McIntire does not require anyway.

### Option C — "GPA-protective" (play for the grade)
| Course | Cr | Why |
|---|---|---|
| COMM 1800 | 3 | Required prereq |
| ENWR 1510 First Writing | 3 | Required prereq |
| ECON 2020 Macroeconomics | 3 | One quant course, not two |
| Language | 3 | Requirement progress, predictable workload |
| Engagements / humanities elective | 3 | Light, GPA-friendly, feeds the writing sample skillset |

**Best if:** fall GPA comes in soft and the spring has to be a repair semester, or he wants deliberate slack for leadership, a business club exec run, or a research/internship push in Feb–April. **Cost:** weakest hedge — a rejection leaves him behind on CS/math sequences.

### Decision rule
1. Check fall midterm grades in **late October 2026**, before spring enrollment opens in November.
2. Quant GPA tracking ≥3.7 → Option A or B. 3.3–3.7 → Option B. Below 3.3 → Option C, and treat spring as a grade-repair semester.
3. Confirm the First Writing status in SIS first — if it's already satisfied, every option gains a free slot; put an Engagements course or a second business-adjacent elective there.
