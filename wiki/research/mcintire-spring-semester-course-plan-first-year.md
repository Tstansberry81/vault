# McIntire Spring Semester Course Plan — First Year

**Prepared for:** Trav, rising second-semester first-year at UVA
**Purpose:** Map out spring semester course selection and a full sequence through application to the McIntire School of Commerce.
**Status:** McIntire is mid-transition to a new curricular model (effective for the cohort starting Fall 2024, which includes Trav). Some official UVA pages still reflect the *old* two-year-entry model, which creates real discrepancies — flagged explicitly below wherever it matters.

---

## 1. Big Finding: McIntire Changed Its Model

McIntire has moved from the old "apply as a second-year, enter as a third-year" model with a long prerequisite list (ECON 2010, ECON 2020, ACCT 2010, STAT 2120, COMM 1800, calculus, FWR) to a **three-year McIntire program** for students entering UVA Fall 2024 and later. Under the new model:

- The prerequisite list is **much shorter**: essentially just **COMM 1800 (Foundations of Commerce)**, **ECON 2010 (Introduction to Microeconomics)**, and the **First Writing Requirement (FWR)**.
- **Accounting (ACCT 2010) and Statistics (STAT 2120)** are **no longer application prerequisites** — they've been moved *into* the McIntire core curriculum, taken *after* a student is admitted, not before.
- Calculus is **not a formal McIntire prerequisite** in the new model (it matters more as general math preparation/Gen Ed than as a gatekeeping course), though it's still smart to have since quant courses in the core lean on it.

**⚠️ Unverified / conflicting sources:** At least one official-looking McIntire/UVA page still lists the old, longer prerequisite set (ECON 2010 + 2020, ACCT 2010, STAT 2120, COMM 1800, calculus, FWR). This looks like stale content that hasn't been fully updated for the transition cohorts. **Trav should confirm directly with a McIntire academic advisor** which list applies to his specific entry year before finalizing his plan — this is the single most important open item in this research.

| Optional 5th course | 3 | Only add if it doesn't compromise performance in the four above — e.g., continue a language sequence, or a second math/quant course if he wants extra rigor signal |

**Load guidance:** 15–16 credits is a sensible, sustainable first-year spring load. Don't stack COMM 1800, a heavy writing seminar, and a hard quant elective all at once if it can be avoided — spread the workload so no single semester has three high-effort, high-reading/writing courses simultaneously.

## 6. Pitfalls to Avoid

1. **Trusting outdated prerequisite lists.** Multiple pages across UVA's own site reflect the *old* two-year-entry prerequisite set (ECON 2010 + 2020, ACCT 2010, STAT 2120, COMM 1800, calculus, FWR). Trav's cohort is under the *new* three-year model with a shorter list — don't accidentally over-plan around retired requirements.
2. **Front-loading now-unnecessary courses.** There's no benefit to rushing ACCT 2010 or STAT 2120 before admission under the new model — they're part of the post-admission core. Taking them early isn't harmful, but it's not a prerequisite win either, so don't sacrifice schedule balance or GPA risk to squeeze them in now.
3. **Taking prerequisites Credit/No Credit (CR/NC).** Even without a confirmed formal minimum-grade rule, admissions is GPA-driven and holistic — CR/NC on COMM 1800, ECON 2010, or the FWR course removes a data point admissions would otherwise use in your favor. Take these for a letter grade.
4. **Overloading the schedule to "get ahead."** Since the new model already lets Trav finish prerequisites a full semester early, there's no need to cram extra business-adjacent courses into spring at the cost of performance. A strong, sustainable GPA matters more than speed here.
5. **Under-using advising during a transition year.** Because the curriculum just changed, there's limited peer precedent (upperclassmen went through the old system). Official McIntire advising is the most reliable, current source of truth — lean on it rather than older forum threads or outdated pages.
