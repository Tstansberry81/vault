# McIntire Spring Semester Course Plan — First Year

*saved by RESOLVE · 2026-07-25 22:40*

## ⚠️ Important framing: McIntire just changed its whole model

McIntire moved from its old **two-year, direct-entry-as-third-year** model to a **three-year program** starting with students who entered UVA in Fall 2024 and later. Trav is in this new cohort. <cite index="6-2">Key changes include earlier application submission options at the end of students' first year and starting the Commerce program at the beginning of their second year, with a focus on reducing prerequisites—requiring the completion of Foundations of Commerce (COMM 1800) and Microeconomics (ECON 2010).</cite> <cite index="16-3">The two online accounting courses have been replaced by an in-person accounting course taken during the first year in Commerce, and the statistics requirement has been removed in lieu of a new quantitative analysis course also taken in the second year.</cite>

This means the prerequisite list is now **much shorter** than the "old" list (ECON 2010+2020, ACCT 2010, STAT 2120, calc, FWR) that circulates in a lot of older advice/forum posts. **Do not plan around the old list** — accounting and stats are now taught inside the McIntire core itself, not required before applying.

## (a) Recommended Spring 2027 Schedule (Trav's first-year spring)

Given Trav will have already finished ECON 2010 in the fall, his spring semester only needs to close out **two** remaining prerequisites (COMM 1800 and the FWR). That leaves 2–3 slots free for GPA-building and gen-ed progress.

| Course | Credits | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **COMM 1800 – Foundations of Commerce** | 3 | Official McIntire prerequisite. <cite index="21-1,21-2">Provides a foundational overview of business, including the history of business, business and society, and innovation, and helps students gauge interest in business.</cite> <cite index="21-6">This course is explicitly a prerequisite for the McIntire School of Commerce.</cite> |
| **FWR-satisfying writing seminar** (e.g., ENWR 1510/1520 or an approved College seminar) | 3–4 | Official prerequisite — <cite index="7-1">the three prerequisites are COMM 1800 "Foundations of Commerce," ECON 2010 "Principles of Economics: Microeconomics," and the first writing requirement.</cite> |
| **ECON 2020 – Principles of Economics: Macroeconomics** | 3 | Not officially required anymore, but strongly recommended: admissions weighs <cite index="12-6">academic performance (GPA, performance in courses related to business, degree of difficulty of courses, and course load taken)</cite>, and macro pairs naturally with the micro he's already taking, keeping his "business-adjacent" GPA strong. |
| **Gen-Ed / Area Requirement course** (Humanities, Social Science, or Second Writing) | 3 | Official guidance: <cite index="19-11">students should continue to make progress on UVA's General Education Requirements</cite> even while completing McIntire prereqs. McIntire's own framing values this too — <cite index="3-3">they're looking for students who have explored and learned about the world through a liberal arts education and who will bring this perspective into their understanding of business.</cite> |
| Optional 5th course / continue Calc sequence | 3–4 | Only add if it doesn't overload him — see pitfalls below. Continuing math (Calc 2) is worthwhile if he's leaning Finance/quant-heavy concentration later, but is **not required** for the application. |

**Target load: 15–16 credits**, which is a normal full course load and leaves room to breathe while knocking out both remaining prereqs.

## (b) Full Verified Prerequisite Checklist (current, 3-year model)

Per the official McIntire admissions pages, only **three** prerequisites now exist:

1. **COMM 1800 – Foundations of Commerce** — not yet taken (plan: Spring 2027)
2. **ECON 2010 – Principles of Economics: Microeconomics** — ✅ **done** (Fall 2026, per his current schedule)
3. **First Writing Requirement (FWR)** — not yet taken (plan: Spring 2027)

<cite index="7-1">These three prerequisites — COMM 1800, ECON 2010, and the first writing requirement — are what's required</cite>, confirmed on McIntire's own admissions site: <cite index="3-18">COMM 1800: Foundations of Commerce, ECON 2010: Principles of Microeconomics, First Writing Requirement.</cite>

Also confirmed: <cite index="8-6,8-7">all prerequisites must be completed by the time a student enters the School in the fall, not by the time the student applies — students who are not enrolled in the prerequisites upon submitting their application must detail on the application how they plan to complete the missing prerequisite course(s) and demonstrate successful progress toward completion.</cite> So Trav is actually in great shape: by the end of spring semester of his first year, all three will be fully complete before he even submits.

## (d) Admissions Criteria & Deadlines

**Evaluation is holistic, not GPA-cutoff based:** <cite index="12-5,12-6,12-7">the Admission Committee reviews each applicant holistically and no one factor guarantees admission — factors include academic performance (GPA, performance in business-related courses, degree of difficulty of courses, course load), co-curricular activities including volunteer/work experience, and evidence of solid communication, teamwork, leadership, and personal skills; students must show both solid academic and co-curricular performance and a level of maturity.</cite>

**Competitive numbers (2026 cycle, most recent verified data):** <cite index="15-1,15-2">the 2026 McIntire admission cycle admission rate declined from last year's 44 percent for first-years applying into the three-year program, with 876 applications meaning about 22 percent of the Class of 2029 applied.</cite> <cite index="15-7,15-8">The mean admitted-student GPA rose from 3.86 the prior cycle to 3.9 in the 2026 cycle, with the overall GPA profile of admitted students trending higher.</cite> Read: **aim for as close to a 3.9+ as possible.**

**Application timing/process (this year's cycle, for reference):** <cite index="12-1,12-2,12-3">the process begins in October for second-year students and March for first-year applicants; second-years have a January deadline while first-year applicants submit at the end of April; the application is available online in the fall.</cite> (Note: this contradicts slightly with the "applications open Jan. 28" language elsewhere — likely because the portal/notification process starts earlier than the live application window. Treat the specific open/close dates from the first-years admissions page as authoritative.)

Outside advising commentary (not official, treat as general strategy color): <cite index="14-1,14-3">grades need to be exceptional, and applicants should aim for top grades particularly in quantitative classes like calculus and economics.</cite> <cite index="14-9,14-10">A vague "I want to study business" doesn't stand out — McIntire is looking for applicants who've already begun to narrow their focus and explore where business meets real-world impact.</cite>

**AP/IB/transfer credit:** I could **not verify current, specific AP/IB substitution rules** for the new 3-prerequisite system from official sources this round. The one official mention found <cite index="2-6">notes that for the Class of 2023 and beyond, AP or transfer credit for STAT 1120 no longer satisfies "the requirement"</cite> — but this is now largely moot since stats isn't a pre-application requirement anymore under the 3-year model. **Flag: confirm with McIntire advising or the UVA transfer-credit equivalency tool whether AP Micro/Macro Econ or AP English scores can satisfy ECON 2010 or the FWR** — general university AP credit policy may apply but wasn't independently confirmed here for McIntire's purposes specifically.

## (e) Pitfalls to Avoid

- **Don't plan around the old prerequisite list.** A lot of advice online (older Reddit threads, some UVA pages not yet updated, transfer guides) still references ACCT 2010, STAT 2120, and ECON 2020 as required before applying. Under the new 3-year model, <cite index="16-2,16-3">the two previously-required online accounting courses have been replaced by an in-person accounting course taken during the first year *in* Commerce, and the statistics requirement has been removed in favor of a new quantitative analysis course also taken in the second year</cite> — i.e., **after** admission, not before.
- **Don't over-cram spring semester** trying to knock out courses that are no longer required — that just adds unnecessary GPA risk with no admissions payoff.
- **Watch the grading mode on prereq courses** — conventional advice (not independently confirmed in an official source this round) is to take COMM 1800, ECON 2010, and FWR for a **letter grade**, not Credit/No Credit, since grades in prereq/business-adjacent courses factor into the "academic performance" evaluation. Confirm this with an advisor before opting into CR/NC on any of these.
- **Don't treat it as just box-checking.** Because the process is explicitly holistic (<cite index="12-5">no one factor guarantees admission</cite>), neglecting extracurriculars, leadership, or work/volunteer experience while chasing a perfect GPA is a common self-sabotage pattern.
- **This is a transition year with less peer wisdom available.** <cite index="18-2,18-3">Because this is the first year under the new admissions process, it's difficult for first-years to be confident about what makes a competitive applicant, and there's less advice from current McIntire students since nobody is fully sure of the new system yet.</cite> Lean on **official McIntire walk-in advising** rather than older forum threads, which may describe the defunct two-year system.
- **Don't wait to sort out FWR.** Get it done in spring exactly as planned — procrastinating on it is one of the most common first-year mistakes since it can back up scheduling in later semesters.
